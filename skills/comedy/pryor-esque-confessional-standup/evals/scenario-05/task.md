# Scenario 05: Draft Where the Narrator Is Only a Victim

## User Prompt

"Here's my bit: I told a story about my landlord raising my rent unfairly and being a terrible person about it. The whole thing is about how awful he was. Does this land?"

## Expected Behavior

1. Recognize that a draft where the narrator is only wronged, with no fault line of their own (foolishness, complicity, fear, being wrong about something), fails the self-implication check in `SKILL.md` § Workflow step 2 and the Troubleshooting guidance.
2. Explain that the piece needs a moment where the narrator was foolish, complicit, or wrong, not just victimized, for the honesty mechanism to work.
3. Propose a concrete revision direction: find a specific beat where the narrator's own behavior (an avoidable mistake, a moment of cowardice, an embarrassing reaction) is part of the story, without erasing the landlord's real wrongdoing.
4. Reference the Mindset principle that the laugh depends on the narrator admitting something true and dangerous about themselves, not just reporting what was done to them.

## Success Criteria

- The response identifies "narrator as pure victim" as the specific craft gap, citing the self-implication requirement.
- The proposed revision direction adds a genuine self-implicating beat rather than just adding more detail about the landlord's behavior.

## Failure Conditions

- The response approves the draft as landing well despite the narrator having no fault line.
- The proposed fix only intensifies how bad the landlord looks, without adding any self-implicating detail about the narrator.
