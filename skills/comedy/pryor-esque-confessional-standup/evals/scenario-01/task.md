# Scenario 01: Original Confessional Bit From a Painful Memory

## User Prompt

"I want to write a stand-up bit about the time I got caught shoplifting as a teenager. It was mortifying but there's something funny in there. Can you help me turn it into a story I could actually perform?"

## Expected Behavior

1. Draft a first-person story grounded in specific, sensory, memory-level detail (a place, an object, an exact reaction) rather than a generic "kid gets caught" setup.
2. Give the narrator a genuine share of the blame or foolishness in the situation, not just bad luck or unfair treatment, the narrator should read as complicit, not purely wronged.
3. Include at least one moment where another person in the scene (security guard, parent, friend, etc.) is embodied in their own distinct voice, entered and exited through rhythm and word choice alone, with no line of narration announcing the switch.
4. Use a short phrase or reaction that recurs two or three times across the story, shifting in intensity or context each time rather than repeating identically.
5. Escalate the stakes or embarrassment so the ending lands harder than the opening implied.
6. Close on a blunt, specific, unresolved observation about the narrator, their family, or their situation, not a stated lesson or moral.
7. Do not draw on, quote, or imitate any real comedian's known material when building the piece.

## Success Criteria

- The draft reads as one continuous first-person story with concrete sensory grounding.
- At least one voice switch is present and legible without being labeled.
- The narrator's own fault is visible in the story, not just what was done to them.
- The story builds rather than stays flat, via escalation and/or a repeated, shifting phrase.
- The ending is a plain, unresolved truth rather than a wrapped-up lesson.

## Failure Conditions

- The story is written as pure observational material with no first-person narrator living through it.
- The narrator is portrayed as an innocent victim throughout, with no admitted fault.
- A voice switch, if present, is announced with a stage-direction label ("in my dad's voice...").
- The story ends with an explicit stated lesson (e.g. "and that taught me to never...").
- Any text is lifted or closely imitated from an actual comedian's real routine.
