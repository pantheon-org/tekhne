# Scenario 05: Draft Escalates Randomly Instead of Logically

## User Prompt

"Here's my sketch draft about a council meeting arguing over a parking permit: it starts with the permit dispute, then suddenly a dragon shows up, then someone becomes a ghost, then it ends. Does this work?"

## Expected Behavior

1. Recognize that jumping from a parking-permit dispute to a dragon to a ghost is randomness, not escalating internal logic — the Mindset section explicitly warns against "escalate the randomness" instead of "escalate the logic."
2. Explain that each absurd turn should follow from the sketch's own established rules (bureaucratic pedantry, semantic argument, literal-minded procedure) rather than introducing unrelated fantastical elements.
3. Propose a revision that keeps escalating the *same* comic engine (e.g. increasingly literal interpretation of permit rules, ever more absurd but procedurally consistent bureaucratic consequences) instead of switching subjects arbitrarily.
4. Reference the specific principle being violated (internally consistent escalation, not random events).

## Success Criteria

- The response identifies the dragon/ghost turns as random rather than logically escalating from the permit premise.
- The proposed fix keeps a single consistent comic engine running through to the end rather than introducing unrelated fantastical elements.

## Failure Conditions

- The response approves the draft as-is, treating the random turns as acceptable "Python-esque randomness."
- The proposed fix adds yet another unrelated random element instead of tightening the escalation to one consistent logic.
