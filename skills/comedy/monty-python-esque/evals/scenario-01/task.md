# Scenario 01: The Chair Definition Dispute

## User Prompt

Write me a short sketch about a council meeting that gets stuck arguing about the definition of
a chair.

## Expected Behavior

1. The agent opens on the ordinary premise (a council meeting) and treats the dispute over what
   counts as a "chair" with total literal seriousness, not as a throwaway aside.
2. The agent drives the scene primarily through pedantry and semantic argument (arguing over the
   definition itself) rather than switching to an unrelated topic.
3. Each new complication in the argument follows logically from the one before it (e.g. arguing
   about arms, legs, or intended use leads to a further, related definitional dispute) rather than
   introducing unrelated strange events to manufacture novelty.
4. Every character stays in deadpan character throughout: no character comments on the scene
   being absurd, breaks the fourth wall, or signals "this is a joke."
5. The sketch does not reuse any real Monty Python sketch's dialogue, character names, or scene
   structure.
6. The sketch ends abruptly (a dry anticlimax, a sudden cut, or a reversal) rather than with a
   tidy resolution, apology, or stated moral.

## Success Criteria

- The core comic engine is clearly pedantry/semantic argument over what qualifies as a chair.
- At least two escalating beats occur, each a logical consequence of the previous one.
- No character breaks deadpan to acknowledge the joke.
- The ending is abrupt/anticlimactic/a reversal, not a wrapped-up resolution.
- No recognisable reused Monty Python material appears.

## Failure Conditions

- The sketch explains why the scene is funny, or has a character say something equivalent to
  "isn't this ridiculous."
- The escalation resets to the same joke repeatedly instead of building, or jumps to unrelated
  randomness (e.g. an unconnected strange event with no logical link to the chair dispute).
- The sketch borrows specific dialogue, a named character, or a scene structure from an actual
  existing Monty Python sketch.
- The sketch ends with an explanatory moral or a neatly resolved outcome.
