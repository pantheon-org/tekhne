# Scenario 02: The Biscuit Complaint

## User Prompt

Can you write a short comic scene where someone tries to make a formal complaint about a biscuit
that snapped unevenly?

## Expected Behavior

1. The agent grounds the scene in an ordinary setting (a complaints desk, an office, a hearing)
   and drives the escalation through bureaucratic procedure (forms, referrals, procedural steps)
   rather than jumping straight to a punchline.
2. The agent optionally mixes pompous, formal register into at least one character's dialogue
   about the trivial biscuit to sharpen the contrast between the seriousness of the language and
   the triviality of the subject.
3. The humour is delivered through understatement: no line narrates or announces that the
   situation is ridiculous.
4. The procedural escalation builds logically (one requirement leads to a further, related
   requirement) rather than resetting or drifting into unrelated randomness.
5. The scene does not reuse dialogue, characters, or structure from any real Monty Python sketch.
6. The scene ends abruptly rather than resolving the complaint neatly or explaining the joke.

## Success Criteria

- Bureaucratic procedure (forms, referrals, a process) is the clear engine driving the scene.
- At least one line uses elevated/formal register applied to the trivial biscuit subject.
- No line editorializes about the situation being absurd or silly.
- The ending is a cut, anticlimax, or reversal, not a resolved complaint or an explained moral.
- No real Monty Python material is reused.

## Failure Conditions

- The scene relies on a punchline-style joke unconnected to escalating procedure.
- A character states or implies "this is ridiculous" directly, breaking understatement.
- The complaint is resolved satisfactorily and the scene ends on a note of closure or an explicit
  lesson learned.
- Any specific real sketch's characters, dialogue, or structure appears.
