# Scenario 02: Blunt wedding-speech bit (provocateur stand-up mode)

## User Prompt

"Write me a short stand-up bit that says something blunt about how fake
wedding speeches are. No need to be nice about it."

## Expected Behavior

1. Recognizes the request as provocateur stand-up mode (direct-address, no
   character or scene needed) and narrows the general topic ("wedding
   speeches") into one specific, falsifiable-sounding claim rather than a
   vague category statement.
2. Drafts an opening line as a single unhedged sentence, stated as settled
   fact, with no softening qualifiers such as "kind of," "I mean," or "I
   could be wrong, but."
3. Follows the opening line with a line that escalates the claim — by
   personalizing it, extending its logic further, or removing an obvious
   excuse — rather than softening or apologizing for it.
4. May optionally include a mock self-congratulation beat, where the narrator
   praises their own honesty or lack of illusions immediately beside the
   least flattering thing they just said.
5. Does not reproduce or closely paraphrase any identifiable real
   comedian's specific lines or routine.
6. Before presenting the bit as final, confirms no hedge, apology, or
   reassurance follows the blunt opening line.

## Success Criteria

- The opening line contains no hedging words and reads as a stated fact
  rather than an opinion offered for debate.
- The line immediately following the opening escalates the claim rather than
  softening it.
- No disclaimer, apology, or "just kidding" follows the blunt claim anywhere
  in the bit.
- The material is original, not a copy or close paraphrase of an
  identifiable real stand-up bit.

## Failure Conditions

- The bit opens with a hedge (e.g. "I don't know, maybe it's just me, but
  weddings feel kind of fake?").
- A line right after the blunt claim reassures or apologizes for it (e.g.
  "but obviously every relationship is different, no judgement").
- The escalation only raises volume or crudeness without extending the
  claim's logic.
- The output matches or closely paraphrases a real, identifiable stand-up
  routine.
