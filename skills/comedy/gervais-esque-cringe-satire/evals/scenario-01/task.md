# Scenario 01: Office holiday toast (character-cringe mode)

## User Prompt

"Can you write me an awkward workplace sketch? I want a manager giving a
holiday toast who thinks he's incredibly funny and inclusive, and the room is
slowly dying inside. Keep it to about a page."

## Expected Behavior

1. Recognizes the request as character-cringe mode (no ambiguity to resolve
   here, since a manager/toast/room-of-onlookers setup is already specific)
   and proceeds without asking unnecessary clarifying questions about mode.
2. Builds the manager around one specific, nameable delusion (e.g. "I'm the
   fun, inclusive boss") rather than a vague "he's not a nice guy" character.
3. Casts one to three onlookers whose reactions are silence, awkward glances,
   or strained politeness rather than dialogue that states outright that the
   manager is being inappropriate.
4. Escalates through at least one recovery-digging beat — the manager trying
   to clarify or prove his point and making it worse — before any
   comeuppance, rather than jumping straight from one misstep to an ending.
5. Keeps the manager unaware of how he is coming across for the entire scene;
   he does not have a line where he suddenly realizes what he sounds like,
   until at most a very final beat.
6. Does not reproduce or closely paraphrase any identifiable real comedian's
   specific lines, characters, or routines; all wording and premise are
   original.
7. Sizes the ending to cost the manager the thing he wanted (being seen as
   fun/inclusive), rather than an unrelated or disproportionately harsh
   outcome (e.g. being fired).

## Success Criteria

- Onlookers react through silence/restraint rather than dialogue that names
  the problem outright.
- The manager shows no moment of self-aware realization before the ending.
- At least one escalating "recovery" beat appears before the ending.
- No line is a copy or close paraphrase of a known real routine.
- The ending costs the manager his self-image (being liked/seen as fun), not
  an unrelated consequence.

## Failure Conditions

- The manager suddenly becomes self-aware and apologizes mid-scene.
- An onlooker explicitly says something like "that was offensive" or
  "I can't believe you just said that."
- The scene contains only one misstep and jumps straight to a resolution with
  no escalation beat in between.
- The output reproduces or closely paraphrases an identifiable real
  comedian's line, character, or routine.
