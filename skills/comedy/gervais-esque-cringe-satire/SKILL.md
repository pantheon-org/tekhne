# Cringe & Provocateur Satire

This skill covers two related modes: character-based cringe comedy (an oblivious
figure surrounded by embarrassed onlookers) and direct-address stand-up that states
an uncomfortable truth bluntly and refuses to soften it. Use whichever mode fits
the request, or blend them — a monologue can open in stand-up mode and cut to a
character-mode flashback, for example.

## Mindset

Both modes work for the same underlying reason: they keep open a gap the audience
wants closed. Cringe comedy withholds the character's self-awareness so the
distance between self-image and reality never resolves; provocateur stand-up
withholds the hedge that would let the audience relax after a taboo claim —
comedy here is discomfort held open on purpose, not defused, and the moment either
mode resolves the discomfort (the character realises, the line gets softened), the
joke stops working.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the request matches either mode:

- **Character-cringe mode** — a request for a sketch, mockumentary scene, script
  excerpt, or short story beat featuring: an authority figure (boss, host, minor
  celebrity, relative giving a toast) who is convinced of a positive trait about
  themselves; onlookers reacting with silence or strained politeness; escalating
  social missteps; a comeuppance.
- **Provocateur stand-up mode** — a request for a stand-up bit, monologue line,
  roast joke, or short blunt riff that states an uncomfortable or taboo claim
  about a solemn subject (death, illness, religion, workplace, identity) without
  cushioning it, then doubles down.
- **Blended requests** — a longer monologue or special-style piece that opens
  direct-address and drops into character voice, or vice versa. Treat this as
  "use both toolkits in sequence," not a third mode.

## When NOT to Use

- The user wants warm, gentle, observational humor with no discomfort or taboo
  element (e.g. "write a cute bit about my dog") — this skill's mechanics will
  overshoot that tone.
- The user wants pure wordplay, puns, or a one-liner with no social-discomfort or
  taboo-statement structure.
- The user asks to reproduce, closely paraphrase, or "write it exactly like"
  a specific real comedian's actual routine, script, or special — see
  Anti-Patterns below; redirect toward original material using these structures
  instead.
- The user wants comedy that resolves reassuringly (a moral, a lesson learned,
  an apology that lands well) — that resolution is the opposite of what these
  mechanics produce; a different comedic register fits better.

## Prerequisites

Before drafting, check the following (ask the user if the request doesn't already make it clear):

- Check which mode fits: character-cringe scene, stand-up bit, or a blend.
- Set the target subject/setting — workplace, family event, funeral, awards
  show, first date, etc. Avoid a vague target ("something awkward"); it
  produces generic filler, so push for specifics.
- Check for any constraints: length, platform (script vs. spoken bit), audience.

## Workflow

**If character-cringe mode:**

1. Build the authority figure around one specific, nameable delusion (see
   `references/character-cringe-craft.md` for how to make the delusion specific
   rather than generic).
2. Add a verbal tic the character thinks is charming; it will recur across
   beats.
3. Include one to three onlookers whose reactions (silence, displaced attention,
   over-polite deflection) will carry the audience's read on the gap — let
   reactions do the work rather than lines that explain the joke.
4. Write an escalation ladder: setup misstep, one or two recovery-digging
   attempts that each extend the same delusion, an overcorrection, then a
   comeuppance sized to cost the character the thing they wanted (being liked,
   seen as cool), not their job or dignity outright.
5. Read the draft back and check that the character never notices the gap
   before the comeuppance — see Verification below.

**If provocateur stand-up mode:**

1. Use a taboo target with widely shared private awareness (death, illness,
   religion, workplace hierarchy, identity), narrowed to a specific claim, not
   a category.
2. Write the opening line as one unhedged sentence stated as settled fact —
   strip qualifiers like "kind of," "I mean," "no offence but."
3. Write a follow-up line that escalates the claim (personalise it, extend the
   logic one step further, or close off the listener's excuse) rather than
   softening it.
4. Add, optionally, a mock self-congratulation beat (narrator praising their
   own tolerance/honesty right beside the least tolerant/honest thing they
   just said).
5. Build toward a closer that refuses a comfortable exit rather than resolving
   the tension, for a longer bit — see `references/provocateur-standup-craft.md`
   for pacing and escalation patterns.
6. Read the draft back and check that no hedge or apology follows the blunt
   line — see Verification below.

## Verification

After drafting, before delivering the material, check the following:

1. Check that the material avoids reproducing any real performer's specific
   lines, characters, or routines (see Anti-Patterns); rewrite any line that
   feels like it could be mistaken for an existing bit, with different
   wording and framing.
2. Verify the character stays unaware of the gap until after the comeuppance
   — no line where they suddenly realise how they sound mid-scene.
3. Verify the line immediately after the blunt statement escalates rather
   than hedges, apologizes, or softens.
4. Read it back once more start to finish: does the discomfort stay unresolved at
   the end (the intended "laugh with a wince"), rather than being tidied up with
   a moral or reassurance?
5. Check that the drafted output is present and complete against every check above before treating it as final; for a longer or ambiguous piece, ask for the user's confirmation on tone before finalizing.

## Troubleshooting

- **The bit reads as mean rather than cringe.** Add a genuine positive trait or
  a plausible self-delusion for the target to be wrong about, so the audience's
  discomfort comes with a flicker of recognition, not just contempt.
- **A stand-up line reads as shock for its own sake, with no laugh.** Add a
  follow-up line that extends the *claim* itself, not just the intensity;
  volume or crudeness without escalating logic is usually the cause.
- **The character explains too clearly why they're wrong.** Remove the
  explanation and let an onlooker's silence carry the meaning instead; that
  level of clarity is self-awareness creeping into the draft.
- **A stand-up bit feels flat and safe.** Check for a hedge word smuggled into the
  opening line ("kind of," "I mean") — strip it and re-read the line alone.

## Core Mechanics Reference

Treat most of the mechanics below as adjustable: PREFER flexible phrasing
("consider," "you can," "either/or") UNLESS a technique is marked **NEVER**
in Anti-Patterns, in which case AVOID any exception to it.

- Build a boss, celebrity, or authority figure who is convinced they are
  broad-minded, clever, or well-liked, and who is completely wrong about how they
  come across.
- Surround that character with onlookers whose silence, awkward glances, or
  strained politeness signal the gap between self-image and reality — let the
  reaction shots do the comedic work rather than dialogue that spells it out.
- Write the character's recovery from a social misstep as further explanation
  that digs the hole deeper with each attempt rather than stopping.
- Write a taboo, uncomfortable, or provocative claim as a short, blunt sentence
  with no hedging, then justify it with a follow-up line that makes it worse
  rather than better.
- Name the thing people are dancing around directly, undercutting politeness
  and euphemism, then look mildly surprised anyone would find that shocking.
- Consider flippant, matter-of-fact delivery for subjects usually treated with
  solemnity, so the mismatch between tone and subject creates the discomfort.
- Optionally build a bit around mock self-congratulation — the character or
  narrator praising their own open-mindedness, tolerance, or cleverness
  immediately after demonstrating the opposite.
- Use long, awkward pauses or silences as punctuation in scripted/character
  material, optionally, rather than filling every beat with dialogue.
- Escalate character bits toward a comeuppance sized to the delusion; escalate
  stand-up bits toward a closer that refuses a comfortable exit — either
  escalation path works, pick whichever the request calls for.
- Keep the audience's discomfort part of the joke rather than resolving it — the
  laugh should come with a wince.

Useful subjects include workplace hierarchy, celebrity vanity, organised religion,
social pretension, and death or illness treated flippantly — this list is a
starting point, not an exhaustive menu; other taboo or status-laden subjects work
by the same mechanics.

### Example: the specific delusion (character mode)

```text
BEFORE (flat, no gap for the audience to see):
MANAGER: I don't care what anyone thinks of me.

AFTER (specific, falsifiable-by-its-own-sentence delusion):
MANAGER: I'm not one of those bosses who needs to be liked.
Everyone likes me anyway, obviously, because I'm hilarious and
completely without ego.
```
What this demonstrates: naming a precise trait ("without ego") inside the same
line that disproves it creates the self-image/reality gap the whole mode depends
on — a vague claim like "I don't care what people think" gives the audience
nothing specific to see through.

### Example: onlooker restraint, not narration (character mode)

```text
BOSS: — and that's why I always say, diversity of thought is what
matters, not diversity of, you know, whatever.
[Beat.]
DEV (not looking up from her laptop): Mm.
```
What this demonstrates: the onlooker's one flat syllable, plus the held beat,
signals the gap without a line that explains what just happened — an onlooker who
says "that's inappropriate" would do the audience's interpretive work for them and
flatten the joke.

### Example: recovery that digs, not deflects (character mode)

```text
MISSTEP: I'm basically an honorary member of every minority in this
building.

RECOVERY THAT WORKS (extends the same delusion):
Which is why I can say it. It's like reverse racism, except good,
because I'm the one saying it.
```
What this demonstrates: the recovery line makes the *same* underlying delusion
more explicit instead of introducing an unrelated flaw — that is what "digging the
hole deeper" means structurally, as opposed to just adding a second, different
joke.

### Example: unhedged claim, then escalation (stand-up mode)

```text
LINE: Weddings are a down payment on a divorce you haven't had yet.

ESCALATION (worsens, does not soften):
And every gift on that registry is basically a get-well card for a
marriage that hasn't gotten sick yet.
```
What this demonstrates: the opening line has no qualifiers and states the claim as
settled fact; the escalation line extends the same logic one step further instead
of softening or apologising for the first line.

### Example: mock self-congratulation (stand-up mode)

```text
I'm probably the most tolerant person you'll ever meet, which is
why I can say with total confidence that your uncle's "spiritual
journey" is a midlife crisis with worse Wi-Fi.
```
What this demonstrates: the self-praise ("most tolerant person") sits directly
beside the least tolerant possible follow-through — the juxtaposition, not the
insult alone, produces the laugh.

### Example: flippant tone on a solemn subject (stand-up mode)

```text
My grandmother's funeral ran forty minutes over, which — for a
woman who was on time for absolutely nothing else in ninety-one
years — feels, if anything, on brand.
```
What this demonstrates: the content is mild; the mismatch between the flat,
logistics-report framing ("ran forty minutes over") and the genuine affection in
"on brand" is what creates the discomfort-plus-laugh combination on its own.

## Anti-Patterns

**NEVER** reproduce a real performer's actual scripts, characters, routines, or
specific lines verbatim or near-verbatim — Ricky Gervais's or anyone else's.
WHY: this is a *generative* skill; its entire value is producing new material
using the underlying structure. Reproducing existing copyrighted lines defeats
that purpose (the user gets a copy, not new material) and creates copyright
exposure.

```text
❌ BAD (near-verbatim reproduction of a known bit's premise and phrasing):
"I'm not a racist. I'm the opposite of a racist. My best friend is
black. David, isn't it? ... David?"

✅ GOOD (same structural device — mock self-congratulation immediately
undercut — original wording and premise):
"I'm about as far from prejudiced as it's possible to be — I once
let a man finish an entire sentence before I decided what I thought
of him."
```
Consequence of skipping this rule: the output is a copy of existing IP rather than
a generated piece, and it can expose the user to a copyright complaint if reused
publicly.

**NEVER** let the oblivious character realize how they come across mid-scene.
WHY: the comedy depends on the gap between the character's self-image and reality
staying open for the audience; a moment of dawning self-awareness during the
escalation closes that gap early and drains the tension the ladder was building.

```text
❌ BAD (self-awareness collapses the gap mid-scene):
MANAGER: I'm basically an honorary member of every— wait, that came
out wrong, didn't it. Sorry. Forget I said that.

✅ GOOD (character stays inside the delusion, oblivious to the room):
MANAGER: I'm basically an honorary member of every minority in this
building. Which is why I can say it. It's like reverse racism,
except good, because I'm the one saying it.
```
Consequence of skipping this rule: the scene shifts from cringe comedy to an
apology beat, and the wince-laugh the mode is built to produce doesn't land.

**NEVER** hedge or apologize immediately after the blunt stand-up line. WHY: the
line's power comes from the removed cushioning; a hedge or apology right after it
puts the cushioning back and softens the exact nerve the line was designed to hit,
turning a joke into a retracted opinion.

```text
❌ BAD (hedge immediately undoes the line):
"Weddings are a down payment on a divorce you haven't had yet. But
obviously that's just me being cynical, most marriages work out
fine, I don't really mean it."

✅ GOOD (the follow-up escalates instead of retreating):
"Weddings are a down payment on a divorce you haven't had yet. And
every gift on that registry is a get-well card for a marriage that
hasn't gotten sick yet."
```
Consequence of skipping this rule: the bit reads as an opinion the speaker
immediately disowns rather than a joke, and the discomfort that was supposed to
carry the laugh gets defused before it lands.
