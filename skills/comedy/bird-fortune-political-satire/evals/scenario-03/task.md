# Scenario 03: Corporate executive briefing on a rebrand after a scandal

## User Prompt

I need a short comedy sketch for a work party. It's a corporate executive giving a briefing about how our totally-not-a-cover-up rebrand is going, after the company got caught doing something dodgy. Make it clever, not just silly.

## Expected Behavior

1. The agent chooses a clear frame (a briefing, given the prompt) and writes it as a scene with the executive addressing an audience/questioner, rather than a plain descriptive paragraph about the rebrand.
2. The executive's language is formal, confident, and evasive — reaching for corporate/procedural vocabulary — rather than silly, absurdist, or slapstick, matching the user's "clever, not just silly" request.
3. The sketch builds across more than one beat: each line or exchange reveals slightly more about the scandal being concealed, rather than making one joke and stopping.
4. The euphemism mechanism is used at least once to redescribe the scandal or the rebrand's real purpose in flattering, procedural terms.
5. The sketch closes on a clear landing (a revealing admission, self-contradiction, or calmly stated absurdity) rather than trailing off without a final beat.
6. The executive is not written as consciously aware they are covering anything up; they present the rebrand as sincerely sensible.

## Success Criteria

- The piece is scene-formatted (frame + dialogue/address), not prose narration.
- The tone stays dry and character-driven rather than silly or slapstick.
- At least one clear escalation step and one clear euphemistic substitution are present.
- There is a definite closing line/beat.

## Failure Conditions

- The sketch is written as narrated description rather than a scene.
- The humour relies on slapstick, absurdist non-sequitur, or random jokes unconnected to the concealment mechanism.
- The executive explicitly acknowledges the rebrand is a cover-up in their own voice (loses sincerity).
- The piece has no discernible ending beat.
