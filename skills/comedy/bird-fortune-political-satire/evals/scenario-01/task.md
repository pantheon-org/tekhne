# Scenario 01: Minister explains a data breach

## User Prompt

Write me a short satirical sketch, two characters, where a government minister is being questioned about a data breach that exposed thousands of people's records. I want it to feel like one of those dry, formal political satire interviews.

## Expected Behavior

1. The agent frames the piece as a formal interview between a journalist/interviewer and the minister, rather than a monologue or narration.
2. The minister's dialogue is written in a confident, polished, circumlocutory register — no crude language and no direct insults from either character.
3. The minister uses euphemism or bureaucratic terminology to describe the breach (e.g. reframing "we lost the data" as some softened, procedural equivalent) rather than plainly admitting the failure in blunt terms.
4. The exchange escalates across at least two or three turns — later lines concede more than earlier ones — rather than repeating the same single joke.
5. The sketch ends on a revealing admission, an elegant self-contradiction, or a calmly stated absurdity, not on an unresolved shrug.
6. The minister never becomes self-aware or winks at the audience about their own hypocrisy — they remain sincere throughout.
7. No dialogue, character names, or premise are copied or closely paraphrased from an actual existing sketch or programme.

## Success Criteria

- Two-character interview format is present and clearly a frame (formal, briefing, or consultation style).
- At least one line of dialogue uses a euphemism for the breach rather than stating it plainly.
- The minister's confidence and self-belief are maintained to the end.
- The sketch has a clear closing beat rather than trailing off.
- All content is original.

## Failure Conditions

- The minister openly admits "yes, I know this is hypocritical/bad" in a way that breaks character self-belief.
- The dialogue uses blunt insults ("you're an idiot," "that's a lie") instead of euphemistic evasion.
- The piece is a monologue or narrated summary rather than an interview exchange.
- Any line is a recognisable near-quote of an existing sketch.
