# Scenario 01: Contrastive bit about class and money

## User Prompt

"Write me a stand-up bit about class and money. I want it to open with a
claim that hits everyone in the room, build through a few examples that
get sharper as it goes, and end on a strong closing line I could actually
quote afterward."

## Expected Behavior

1. Opens with a single bold, sweeping claim about class or money, framed as a "there's a difference between X and Y" contrast rather than a list of separate observations.
2. Provides a sequence of concrete, everyday examples (not abstract statements) that escalate in specificity or social risk from the first example to the last.
3. Voices one brief imagined objector or counter-argument partway through and knocks it down before continuing to build.
4. Closes on a single, tight, quotable sentence that reframes the whole bit, with no hedge, disclaimer, or qualifier attached after it.
5. Produces entirely original wording and claims — does not lift or closely paraphrase any real comedian's existing material.

## Success Criteria

- The bit is structured around one contrastive claim, not a set of unrelated jokes.
- Every example is a concrete scene or image, not an abstract statement of the claim.
- The escalation is visible: later examples read as more pointed than earlier ones.
- The closing line stands alone, unqualified.
- No example or line matches or closely echoes a known existing routine.

## Failure Conditions

- The bit is a flat list of one-liners about class/money with no unifying contrast or escalation.
- Examples remain generic or abstract ("rich people act differently than poor people") with no concrete scene.
- The ending line is followed by a softening caveat ("but hey, that's just my opinion").
- The output reproduces or very closely paraphrases an identifiable existing bit.
